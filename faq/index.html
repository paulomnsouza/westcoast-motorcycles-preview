Finance

How can I get finance for a motorcycle?

You must be 18 years or older, currently employed and have no outstanding defaults on your credit file. For registered bikes you need at least a motorcycle learner’s permit. We do the application in store and it takes about 15 minutes.

What do I need to bring for a finance application?

Two payslips (at least one dated within the last 30 days), photo ID such as a driver’s licence or passport, and a bank statement or official bank form showing your BSB, account number, name and a date within the last 30 days.

What finance rate will I get?

Our in-house motorcycle finance currently has some very low rates. The rate is set by the finance company and reflects your credit risk. Ask us about 12-month zero percent finance.

Buying a motorcycle

Do I need a current WA driver’s licence to buy a motorcycle?

You need a WA licence to buy a registered bike, or photo ID and proof of a residential address in WA. Interstate customers can buy unregistered bikes. You can buy an unregistered bike with no licence, and you can buy a licensed bike with an incorrect class of licence.

How can I pay for my bike?

We accept most bank cards, bank cheques, cash and direct transfers, and we can arrange finance. We can also set up a lay-by, where we hold the bike while you pay it off.

Do you insure bikes you didn’t sell?

Yes. We can arrange insurance for almost any model, new or used – you don’t need to have bought the bike from us.

Servicing & maintenance

What regular maintenance does a motorcycle need?

Basic weekly maintenance: check all fluids, lube the chain, check tyre pressures, adjust chain tension, and clean and lubricate the bike.

What are scheduled services?

Every motorcycle has a service schedule in its owner’s manual – or call our service centre. Most road bikes need a service once a year or every 6,000 km.

What tyre pressures should I run?

Motorcycles rely on the profile of the tyre for handling, so correct pressures matter. A road bike will generally run around 35 psi front and 42 psi rear, but this varies from bike to bike – check your manual or ask our tyre centre.

Do you service bikes you don’t sell?

Yes. Our workshop welcomes all makes, including KTM, Yamaha, Kawasaki, Suzuki, Triumph, BMW, Beta, Sherco and many more.

Riding gear & licences

What protective clothing do I need to ride on the road?

Legally you only need an approved helmet that meets Australian standards. To be safe, our staff can help you choose proper protective gear from head to toe for every type of weather.

Where can I get motorcycle lessons?

For motorcycle lessons, contact Motorcycle Training WA on 0426 008 969. For licensing questions, call us on (08) 9300 0094.

Types of motorcycles

What are the main motorcycle categories?

Super sports: based on road-racing bikes, perfect for track days. Sports: friendlier for general road use. Naked: no fairings, great for city riding. Cruiser: relaxed riding position for open roads. Touring and sports touring: built for comfort over long and mid distances. Road trail: everyday road bikes with some off-road ability. Adventure touring: larger bikes for on- and off-road travel. Competition/MX: built for motocross. Enduro: competition-based with wider gearing and softer suspension. Fun bikes: low-maintenance off-road bikes for new riders.
